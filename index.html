About

I trained as an electronics engineer before moving into radio astronomy and cosmology. I'm currently a PhD student at the Institute of Astronomy, University of Cambridge, working at the intersection of radio astronomy, Bayesian statistics, and quantum computing to understand how the first stars and galaxies reionized the Universe.

Day to day, that means debugging numerical instabilities in the REACH calibration pipeline, modelling X-ray binary populations for 21-cm simulations, and running quantum circuits to simulate fuzzy dark matter solitons. Outside work I sing, cook, and spend a lot of clear nights looking at the same sky I study for a living.

Research

REACH Calibration Numerical stability Bayesian inference

Numerical instabilities in the REACH calibration pipeline

PhD research, University of Cambridge — supervisor: Dr. Eloy de Lera Acedo

REACH's Bayesian calibration framework occasionally fails in ways that are hard to pin down. I'm tracking down where those failure modes come from and building corrections so the instrument's absolute calibration holds up for precision 21-cm signal recovery.

X-ray binaries Statistical modeling 21-cm simulations

X-ray binary luminosity distributions and 21-cm simulations

PhD research, University of Cambridge — supervisors: Prof. Anastasia Fialkov & Dr. Boyuan Liu

Looking at how X-ray binary luminosities are distributed, using statistical models that capture how stochastic these populations really are, and feeding that into 21-cm simulations of the Cosmic Dawn and Epoch of Reionization.

Quantum computing Fuzzy dark matter IQAE Qiskit

Quantum simulation of fuzzy dark matter solitons

PhD research, University of Cambridge

Simulating fuzzy dark matter soliton evolution with hybrid quantum-classical circuits — Trotter-decomposed circuits for the Schrödinger-Poisson system, and Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation for density readout, which gives a quadratic speedup over direct sampling. As far as I know this is the first use of IQAE for density extraction in a quantum cosmological simulation.

Exotic 21-cm signals DEX ALO Detectability

Exotic 21-cm signals and detectability with DEX/ALO

PhD research, University of Cambridge

Working out what non-standard-physics 21-cm signal scenarios would look like, and whether the DEX and ALO experiments could actually detect them.

Line intensity mapping CII CO 21-cm code

Line intensity mapping with CII/CO in 21-cm simulations

Collaborative project

Adding CII and CO line intensity mapping to a standard 21-cm simulation codebase, so we can cross-correlate different emission lines with the 21-cm signal.

21-cm cosmology SKA Machine learning Bayesian inference

ML-assisted Bayesian pipeline for CD-EoR 21-cm images

MS thesis, IIT Indore — supervisors: Dr. Suman Majumdar & Prof. Abhirup Datta

Built a machine-learning-assisted Bayesian pipeline for interpreting the Cosmic Dawn / EoR 21-cm tomographic images the SKA will produce, using Largest Cluster Statistics to trace the topology of ionized hydrogen regions back to properties of the ionizing sources and IGM.

SKA Data challenge Radio astronomy

SKA Science Data Challenge 3

SKA Science Working Group

Working with realistic synthetic SKA datasets to develop analysis techniques for the order-of-magnitude jump in sensitivity the next generation of radio telescopes will bring.

Deep learning CNN 21-cm

Neutral fraction prediction from HI 21-cm images

Astro Hack Week 2022, Max Planck Institute for Astronomy

Trained a CNN to predict the neutral fraction (xHI) directly from HI 21-cm images — a faster stand-in for the usual analysis pipeline.
